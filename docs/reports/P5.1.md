## Outcome and scope

Done locally under DEC-003 ("Stay free, fixtures only"). The 40 fictional documents are real PDFs, made by script with fixed dates so the bytes never change, and their text is extracted once at build time with `unpdf` in Node. The text is stored as page-numbered segments and seeded into a shared read-only table. A person attaches a document by its allowlisted fixture ID, and every document row carries its file's SHA-256, size and type. The owner upload route authorizes first, checks the bytes without parsing them, and then always refuses. No object is stored anywhere and no bucket exists.

Not attempted, by decision: hosted upload, R2, any PDF parsing in the Worker, OCR.

## Security, quality and cost

- The byte checks are not a PDF parser and don't claim a file is safe; they refuse files that are plainly wrong or plainly active, and the route refuses everything else anyway.
- The fixture check scans the extracted text in the manifest for real-identifier patterns; the PDFs themselves are held to it by hash, because PDF offsets look like card numbers to the scanner.
- New dev dependencies: `pdf-lib` 1.17.1 and `unpdf` 2.1.0, both free and open source, build-time only, not in the Worker bundle. `npm audit`: 0 vulnerabilities.
- Cost: $0.


## Independent review follow-up

A document can no longer be attached to a case that is Ready, Approved or Closed (`case_locked`), matching the rule that what a reviewer approved stays what was approved. The upload byte scan does not decode hex-escaped PDF names such as `/J#61vaScript` and does not refuse `/Encrypt`; that is acceptable only because the route always refuses, and it must not be described as detection. If uploads are ever enabled, decode `#xx` and refuse encryption first.
